Lessons & Talks

This space offers teachings drawn from Tibetan Buddhist traditions — not as tools for self-optimization, but as invitations into deeper awareness, compassion, and liberation. Rather than quick fixes or marketable routines, these lessons explore timeless concepts such as impermanence, non-attachment, the nature of suffering, and the practice of loving-kindness. They’re not about escaping the world, but learning to meet it — and ourselves — with clarity and care.

What You’ll Find Here:

1. Introductory Teachings For those new to Buddhism, simple and clear overviews of key ideas like the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, and meditation basics.

2. Talks by Practitioners and Teachers Recorded or transcribed talks reflecting on Buddhist ethics, daily practice in urban life, and the challenges of living compassionately in a capitalist culture.

3. Critical Reflections Thought pieces exploring how mindfulness is often co-opted by corporate wellness culture, and how we might return to a more rooted, communal, and liberatory understanding of practice.

4. Contemplative Readings Short texts, quotes, or readings from the Tibetan tradition to reflect on, alone or with others.

All offerings are freely available, shared in the spirit of the Dharma: open-handed, without cost or obligation. Whether you're just beginning or returning to these teachings, may they be of benefit to you and those around you.

🌬️ 1. Mindful Breathing (Shamatha) Focus: Calm-abiding Practice Length: 5–20 minutes Description: This foundational practice centers on the breath — not to escape life, but to return to it. By simply observing the natural inhale and exhale, we learn to rest the mind, notice distractions without judgment, and gently return to the present moment. Shamatha builds stability and focus, helping us soften the nervous system and meet life with clarity.

💗 2. Loving-Kindness Meditation (Metta) Focus: Cultivating unconditional goodwill Practice Length: 15–30 minutes Description: In this heart-centered practice, we silently offer kind wishes — first to ourselves, then outward to others. Starting with “May I be safe. May I be peaceful. May I be free,” the circle expands to include friends, strangers, and even those we find difficult. This practice is not about forcing love, but uncovering the natural compassion beneath the surface of judgment.

🌫️ 3. Meditation on Impermanence & Detachment Focus: Letting go Practice Length: 10–30 minutes Description: This contemplative practice invites us to sit with the truth of change. Using imagery, breath, or reflection on life’s transience, we begin to loosen the grip of attachment — to outcomes, people, even identity. This is not about apathy; it’s about freedom: seeing clearly that everything arises and passes, and that peace is found in letting go.

🌌 4. Open Awareness (Dzogchen-style inspired) Focus: Resting in the natural state Practice Length: 15–45 minutes Description: Rather than focusing on one object, this practice invites us to rest in spacious awareness itself — open, vast, without clinging to thought or sensation. Common in Tibetan Dzogchen and Mahamudra traditions, this meditation supports insight into the mind’s true nature and helps us move beyond effort into simply being.
