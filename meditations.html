🧘‍♀️ Types of Meditation Practices

🌬️ 1. Breathing Meditation (Mindfulness of Breath) A practice of anchoring the mind in the present moment through the breath. This simple but profound meditation uses the breath as a focal point. Observing each inhale and exhale helps calm mental chatter and steady attention. Over time, this cultivates clarity, emotional balance, and a gentle return to presence.

🦶 2. Body Scan Meditation A journey through the body to reconnect with sensation and presence. This practice involves moving awareness slowly through different parts of the body, noticing without judgment. It promotes grounding, stress release, and a deeper sense of embodiment — ideal for calming anxiety or restlessness.

🕊️ 3. Loving-Kindness Meditation (Metta Bhavana) A heart-opening practice that cultivates unconditional kindness. Through silently repeating phrases like “May I be well,” and extending these wishes to others, we soften harshness and grow compassion — not just as a feeling, but as a way of being. This practice helps transform fear, anger, and disconnection.

🧍 4. Standing Meditation Finding rootedness and inner stillness while upright and alert. Often used in Zen and Qigong traditions, standing meditation grounds awareness in the body while allowing alertness to remain. It's especially beneficial if you're tired or distracted while sitting, or if you want to energize your practice.

🏔️ 5. Mountain Meditation A guided visualization that evokes the strength and stillness of a mountain. In this imagery-based meditation, you embody the mountain’s grounded presence — unshaken by weather, rooted through stillness, and spacious in awareness. It’s a powerful reminder of the stability we carry within, even in life’s storms.

🚶 6. Walking Meditation Bringing mindfulness into motion, one step at a time. Practiced slowly and intentionally, walking meditation connects body and breath with movement. Each step becomes an opportunity to arrive — fully present, grounded, and awake. Perfect for those who find stillness easier while moving.

🌫️ 7. Open Awareness (Choiceless Awareness) Letting go of focus to rest in the natural openness of the mind. Instead of concentrating on one object, this practice invites you to rest in awareness itself — open, spacious, and free. Thoughts, sounds, sensations all come and go, but you remain the witness. This supports insight into impermanence and selflessness.

🔥 8. Tonglen (Compassionate Breathing) Breathing in suffering, breathing out relief — a radical act of compassion. In Tonglen, we imagine inhaling the pain of others, and exhaling healing or peace. This counterintuitive yet powerful Tibetan practice helps break down self-centeredness and awaken deep empathy.
